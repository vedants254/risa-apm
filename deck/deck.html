Problem Statement

Run a major college fest in a Tier 1 Indian city and maximise the number of people who actually attend, on limited time, budget and team. Defend the highest target you can, as a base and a stretch.

How High
Can We Aim?
8,000 verified attendees for ₹10.2L (₹130 each), and 16,000 if early signals earn a ₹6.5L top-up. CampusFest 2025 drew 2,050 on ₹2.74L. The jump comes from moving spend to channels whose registrants actually show up.

Stretch needs: 160 partners × 36 registrations (base 120 × 25), 280 ambassadors × 25 (base 200 × 20), a 10% reconfirmation lift, and the ₹6.5L released only at T-21 if pace and cost pass slide 3.

Why Believe 8,000

54% of 20,000 simulated plans reach the base. It is 1.1% of the 7 lakh students in reach, while peer fests get 1.5–3%. Budget bound: ₹11L ÷ ₹104, the 2025 cost per visitor in the five best channels.

How the three numbers are set. The funnel model gives 7,842 verified attendees for ₹10.2L, which we round to 8,000. With the stretch inputs it gives 15,973 for ₹17.3L, rounded to 16,000. The venue holds 15,000 people a day. We cap passes at 90%, the fest runs two days and 35% of people come on both, so 15,000 × 90% × 2 ÷ 1.35 = 20,000 unique attendees at most.

Capped, kill switch
6,320 from the four channel bets + 1,044 host college and search + 478 walk-ins = 7,842 attendees for ₹10.2L of the ₹11L budget, ₹130 each. The show-up engine's +374 is already inside the channel rates.

Why this order: bets 1–3 are cheap and proven in the 2025 data, so we double down. Ambassadors bring the most volume but cost more per attendee. Paid stays capped and can be switched off in a week.

1

Show-up engine

Reconfirm every registrant on WhatsApp at T-7 and T-3, issue a named QR pass, and release unclaimed seats at T-2.

0 of 300

registrants unconfirmed at T-1 attended in 2025, while 26 of 36 who reconfirmed did.

Test: A/B on the first 1,000 registrants

KPI

  • share confirmed by T-3
  • show-up gap vs control
2

College & society partnerships

Sign college societies and fest committees. Members register together on a group pass, with return transport for NCR colleges.

86% show-up

and ₹69 per registration in 2025, the best of all seven channels.

Test: 15 pilot societies in weeks 1–2

KPI

  • signed partners
  • group commitments per partner
3

Referral squads

Every registrant gets a link. A squad of three registered friends gets fast-lane entry.

₹56 / reg

the cheapest 2025 channel, and it costs nothing until a friend registers.

Test: reward A/B: fast-lane vs merch

KPI

  • invites per registrant
  • k-factor
4

Campus ambassadors

200 ambassadors across Delhi and NCR colleges, paid per verified attendee instead of per sign-up.

2,678

attendees, the largest single source, with 75% show-up in 2025.

Test: 50-ambassador pilot: per-attendee pay vs stipend

KPI

  • active share
  • registrations per ambassador a week
5

Warm paid retargeting

Retarget warm audiences only (site visitors, form starters) and hold 70% of the paid budget for the last 30 days.

₹67 vs ₹126

per registration in the last 30 days vs before. 47.5% of 2025 registrations came then.

Test: weekly geo holdout

KPI

  • cost per registration
  • landing page → registration
What We Won't Do, and Why
Creator and influencer deals
➜

Worst 2025 channel: ₹337 per visitor and 50% show-up.

Nurture email to unregistered leads
➜

700 emailed, 75% opened, 0 registered.

Cold paid-social prospecting
➜

The only channel that got less efficient late in the season (0.67×), with 50% show-up.

A celebrity pro-night as the hook
➜

The fee would exceed the marketing budget and puts the crowd risk at the gate.
